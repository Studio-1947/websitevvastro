Where It Started

It began as the Himal Nagarik Fellowship.

The first cohort hired nine fellows from the hills around us: no design portfolio required, no computer-science degree, just a willingness to learn. Three of them stayed on with Studio 1947 in full job roles once the fellowship ended, the clearest sign the model was working. It has grown since, but the idea hasn't changed: give local talent real exposure and a real stipend, not an unpaid internship dressed up as an opportunity.

What Fellows Get

Technology

Exposure to the tools and build processes that turn a design into something that actually ships.

Communication

Learning how a studio talks to clients and communities, and how a project gets explained clearly to both.
